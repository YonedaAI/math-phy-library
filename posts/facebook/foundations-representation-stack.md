🧩 When a physicist says a quantity is “given by” some piece of math — a cohomology class, a modulus, an index — what does that arrow from math to measurement actually mean? And how sure should we be?

Most of the time, nobody says. The identification of math with physical reality is usually left as an unstated assumption.

💡 Part I of a new seven paper modular series tackles this head on. It builds a “representation stack” — a rigorous pipeline that takes a mathematical object, translates it through a named physical channel, and reads off an observable number: Obs(M) = Obs(Real(Φ(M))).

Every single step in that pipeline carries a confidence label — Standard, Heuristic, or Speculative — and the paper proves these labels behave predictably: confidence can only go down, never up, as you chain translations together. No free epistemic lunches.

🔬 Why it matters: this gives researchers a shared, checkable scaffold for being honest about how solid a “math equals physics” claim really is, instead of quietly assuming it. It is Part I of a modular (not unified!) program, meaning each of the next six papers builds its own mathematical faculty on top of this foundation, brick by brick.

The paper has been peer reviewed, code reviewed, and its core structures are verified in both Haskell and Lean.
