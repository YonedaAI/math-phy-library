🔍 Ever wonder why particle physics calculations — the messy integrals behind scattering amplitudes — keep producing oddly clean, algebra-like patterns when you look at their cuts and discontinuities?

It is not a coincidence. And now there is a precise mathematical reason for it.

💡 Part II of our modular seven paper series shows that scattering amplitudes carry a hidden arithmetic skeleton called a motive, and that its coaction — a kind of structured “splitting rule” from motivic Galois theory — transports exactly onto the decompositions physicists already compute by hand for cuts, discontinuities, and residues.

⚠️ Honesty matters here too. The paper also proves a genuine limit: certain more exotic mathematical objects (elliptic and other “non Tate” motives) provably escape this tidy algebraic picture. Not everything reduces to the simple polylogarithm story, and now we know exactly where the line is.

🔬 Why it matters: this gives physicists a rigorous arithmetic explanation for patterns long treated as folklore in amplitude calculations, while being precise about where the method stops working.
