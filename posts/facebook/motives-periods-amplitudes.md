If you have ever stared at a scattering amplitude calculation, the messy integrals behind how particles bounce off each other, you might have noticed something odd. Look at their cuts and discontinuities and weirdly clean, almost algebraic patterns keep showing up.

That is not a coincidence, and Part II of our modular seven paper series gives the reason. A scattering amplitude carries a hidden arithmetic skeleton called a motive. The motive comes with a coaction, a structured splitting rule out of motivic Galois theory, and that coaction lands exactly on the decompositions physicists already work out by hand for cuts, discontinuities, and residues.

We also drew the boundary honestly. The paper proves that some more exotic objects, elliptic and other non-Tate motives, genuinely escape this tidy picture. Not everything collapses to the simple polylogarithm story, and now we can say precisely where that story runs out.

So physicists get a real arithmetic reason for patterns that were treated as folklore in amplitude work, together with an honest map of where the method stops.

Part II of a modular (not unified) seven paper program, peer reviewed, code reviewed, and checked in Haskell and Lean.
