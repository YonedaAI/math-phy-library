If you have taken a bit of topology you have probably heard that a topological quantum field theory is "basically a functor." It usually gets said and then dropped, like a slogan nobody cashes in.

Part IV of our modular seven paper series actually cashes it in. We treat algebraic topology as the bookkeeping of conserved global information, the quantities that survive when you deform something continuously. Homology tracks the stable observables, characteristic classes carry the quantized topological data, and a TQFT turns out to be exactly a representation entry in our formal framework. That last part discharges one of the framework's core axioms outright.

The Atiyah-Singer index theorem falls out the same way. It is often called one of the deepest results linking geometry and analysis, and in our setup it reads as a straight instance of the "observable equals realization of abstract structure" pipeline. Not an analogy to it. The same statement.

This is the first module in the series where that representation pipeline idea gets proven rather than proposed, so it works as a solid case study before the more speculative modules that lean on it.

Part IV of a modular seven paper program, peer reviewed, code reviewed, and checked in Haskell and Lean.
