🧩 Six independent research papers, each building a different piece of mathematics into a way of representing physics. Do they actually fit together, or is that just a nice claim?

Our capstone synthesis paper proves that they do — and shows exactly how.

💡 The papers build a “composition ladder”: motives and periods, then algebraic geometry, then algebraic topology, then category theory and homotopy type theory, then sheaves and stacks. At each step a new emergent property shows up — but only after you actually compose the pieces, never assumed ahead of time.

🏆 The centerpiece is a Closure Theorem: the “representation stack” proposed loosely back in paper one turns out to be a real, provable mathematical object, closed under everything the other six papers do to it. And its keystone formula — connecting a stack's automorphisms to a stabilizer group — gets proved FOUR completely independent ways across the series. Its physical meaning: gauge redundancy in physics and fault tolerant quantum encoding are the same structure.

🔬 Why it matters: this is explicitly a modular framework, not a single unified theory. Each of the six papers stands on its own, and the coherence between them is something we track and prove, not something we simply assert.

Across all seven papers: peer reviewed, code reviewed, and formally verified in Haskell and Lean, with 268 individually status-labeled dictionary entries.
