Six separate research papers, each wiring a different area of mathematics into a way of representing physics. The obvious worry is whether they actually fit together or whether "they fit together" is just something you say at the end.

Our capstone synthesis paper shows they do fit, and it shows the joins. The papers stack into a composition ladder: motives and periods, then algebraic geometry, then algebraic topology, then category theory and homotopy type theory, then sheaves and stacks. Every rung adds a new emergent property, but only once you have genuinely composed the pieces below it. Nothing is assumed in advance.

The heart of it is a Closure Theorem. The representation stack that paper one only sketched loosely turns out to be a real, provable object, closed under everything the other six papers throw at it. Its keystone formula, the one linking a stack's automorphisms to a stabilizer group, gets proved four completely independent ways over the course of the series. What that formula means physically: gauge redundancy in physics and fault tolerant quantum encoding are the same structure.

I want to be clear this is a modular framework, not one grand unified theory. Each of the six papers stands on its own, and the coherence between them is something we track and prove rather than assert.

Across all seven papers: peer reviewed, code reviewed, and formally verified in Haskell and Lean, with 268 individually status-labeled dictionary entries.
