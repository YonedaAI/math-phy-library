Why can you not clone a qubit? Physics textbooks usually just hand you the no-cloning theorem as a postulate of quantum mechanics and move on.

Part V of our modular seven paper series derives it instead, starting from pure category theory with no physics assumed. In a certain kind of category (the jargon is "dagger compact"), asking for a uniform, natural way to copy any object forces the whole category to collapse into a classical, cartesian one. Uniform copying and quantum behavior cannot coexist, and that incompatibility is the structural root of no-cloning.

We also spend time with homotopy type theory's univalence axiom, the principle that equivalent things really are equal rather than merely related, and show it promotes one of our earlier assumptions into an honest theorem.

This is the paper where the whole series gets glued together. The rules for chaining translations coherently get spelled out here, which is what forces the quantum error correction module later on to use genuine embeddings instead of plain copies.
