🧬 Why can you not clone a qubit? Physics textbooks usually just state the no cloning theorem as a postulate of quantum mechanics.

Part V of our modular seven paper series derives it instead — from pure category theory, with no physics assumed at all.

💡 We show that in a certain kind of category (called “dagger compact”), simply requiring a uniform, natural way to copy things forces that category to collapse into a classical, cartesian structure. Uniform copying and quantum behavior are mathematically incompatible. That is the structural root of no cloning.

🪞 We also look at homotopy type theory's univalence axiom — the principle that equivalent things ARE equal, not just related — and prove it upgrades an earlier assumption in our framework into an actual theorem.

🔬 Why it matters: this paper is the compositional glue of the whole series. It is where the abstract rules for “chaining translations together coherently” get made explicit, explaining why the quantum error correction module that follows must use genuine embeddings rather than simple copies.
