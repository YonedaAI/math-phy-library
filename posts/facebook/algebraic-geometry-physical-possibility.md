🌌 What is the actual mathematical space of “everything a physical system could possibly be”?

Physicists use phrases like “moduli space” and “space of configurations” all the time. But different mathematical descriptions of that same space can quietly throw away real physical information — and most people never notice.

💡 Part III of our modular seven paper series makes this precise. A classical solution space is a variety. Add infinitesimal wiggle room and it becomes a scheme. A space of genuinely different physical setups is a moduli space. And when you keep track of each configuration's gauge symmetries instead of dividing them away, you get a moduli STACK — a strictly richer object.

📐 We prove exactly when the “stack” version carries more information than the plain quotient: whenever a configuration has nontrivial symmetry (a nonzero stabilizer). That is not a philosophical point, it is a theorem.

🔬 Why it matters: this gives a rigorous test for when a common simplification in physics — just take the quotient — is actually throwing information away, and builds the geometric foundation for the topology paper that follows.
