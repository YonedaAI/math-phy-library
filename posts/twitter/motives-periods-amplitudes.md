Why do Feynman integrals have cuts, discontinuities, and residues that look almost algebraic? Part II of the modular math→physics series argues scattering amplitudes carry a hidden arithmetic structure: a coalgebra.

2/4
We formalize a math object's "motive" as what it does functionally, realize it through four cohomology theories (Betti, de Rham, Hodge, étale), and show the period map decomposes amplitudes exactly as the coproduct predicts.

3/4
The coaction on an amplitude's motivic double transports through realization into the decomposition physicists compute by hand for cuts and discontinuities. There's also a hard limit: non-Tate motives (elliptic, say) fall outside this polylog anatomy.
