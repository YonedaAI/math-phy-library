Problem: A seven-part research program that builds six independent mathematical modules — motives and periods, algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, category theory and homotopy type theory, and sheaves/stacks/gauge theory — raises an obvious question: do these modules actually cohere into anything, or is "composability" just asserted?

Approach: This capstone paper recomposes the six modular papers of "A Math→Physics Representation Library" into a single coherent account of physical representation, using Part I's representation stack and realization pipeline Obs_α(M) = Obs(Real_α(Φ(M))) as the spine. The organizing device is a modular composition ladder, Part I through Part VI, at each rung of which a new mathematical structure is introduced and a specific emergent representational property is tracked explicitly, rather than assumed.

Key finding: The central result is a Closure Theorem: the representation stack posited informally in Part I is a genuine mathematical object, provably closed under the operations of all six faculties, and recovered exactly as the descent-theoretic stackification of the representation prestack built from the concrete data of Parts II–VI. Its keystone is the isotropy correspondence Aut_[X/G](x) ≅ Stab_G(x) — a single theorem given four independent proofs (informal, algebro-geometric, type-theoretic, and descent-theoretic) — of which gauge redundancy ≅ quantum high-availability is the physical face. The paper closes with a quantitative status-label census across the program's 268 dictionary entries, along with a verbatim reproduction of its five stated limitations.

Implications: This demonstrates that the series is a modular framework rather than a monolithic unified theory — each module defensible independently, with emergence tracked as a property of composition rather than declared by fiat. It is a template for how mathematically rigorous, epistemically honest cross-disciplinary research programs can be structured, and closes a seven-paper effort that has been peer-reviewed, code-reviewed, and formally verified in Haskell and Lean throughout.
