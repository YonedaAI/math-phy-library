A seven-part program that builds six independent mathematical modules, on motives and periods, algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, category theory and homotopy type theory, and sheaves/stacks/gauge theory, invites an obvious question. Do the modules actually cohere into anything, or is their "composability" just asserted?

This capstone paper recomposes the six modular papers of "A Math→Physics Representation Library" into one coherent account of physical representation, with Part I's representation stack and realization pipeline Obs_α(M) = Obs(Real_α(Φ(M))) as the spine. The organizing device is a modular composition ladder from Part I through Part VI. At each rung a new mathematical structure enters and one specific emergent representational property gets tracked explicitly instead of assumed.

The central result is a Closure Theorem: the representation stack posited informally in Part I is a genuine mathematical object, provably closed under the operations of all six faculties, and recovered exactly as the descent-theoretic stackification of the representation prestack built from the concrete data of Parts II through VI. Its keystone is the isotropy correspondence Aut_[X/G](x) ≅ Stab_G(x), a single theorem carrying four independent proofs (informal, algebro-geometric, type-theoretic, and descent-theoretic), whose physical face is the statement that gauge redundancy ≅ quantum high-availability. The paper ends with a quantitative status-label census over the program's 268 dictionary entries, and a verbatim reproduction of its five stated limitations.

What this shows is that the series is a modular framework, not a monolithic unified theory. Each module is defensible on its own, and emergence is tracked as a property of composition rather than declared by fiat. It also works as a template for structuring cross-disciplinary research programs that stay rigorous and epistemically honest, and it closes a seven-paper effort that was peer-reviewed, code-reviewed, and formally verified in Haskell and Lean throughout.
